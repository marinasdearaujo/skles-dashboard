The strategy in brief

Reach wide, warm the hot ones, keep the members

Cold audience meets SKLES through organic content and paid ads, and is captured with a free ebook. The recorded Fast Track webinar then introduces the whole system to a broad audience, relational in tone, scalable, no live host each time.

Leads who warm up get the live relational meeting, a more personal room for people who haven't bought yet but are close. It nudges the hottest leads toward Apprentice, and it keeps existing members in the room too.

August validated the bet: the first member came through this path, and the ebook engine beat its cost target.

What the numbers say

The story the numbers tell together

August was the month the funnel finally captured. The ebook turned $773 into 30 leads at $25.77 each, from a page that recorded zero the month before. The top of the funnel is healthy on its own: TikTok pulled 12,000 views almost entirely from strangers, while LinkedIn barely reached its own 7,270 followers, and YouTube has the volume but holds only 29 seconds of attention per view.

Then the ramp splits. The recorded webinar did the converting, 8 registered and 1 bought, with no one needing to show up live. The live meeting is the opposite so far: 14 registered, 3 showed, none bought. The no-shows wrote "time freedom", "family", "because I'm broke". What leaks is the step between: the guide-delivery email opens at just 17.7% and its follow-ups drew no clicks, and while broadcasts open at a healthy 36%, the webinar invitations produced 5 clicks across 507 sends. People are reading and not acting.

Across every layer the pattern is the same: attention isn't the problem, conversion is. The reach, the opens, the ad click-through, all above benchmark. The leak is at the ask, the form, the email CTA, the show-up, and that's copy and mechanics, not spend.
