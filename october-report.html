Where we are

The ads worked. The step after the form did not.

October spent almost exactly what September spent, ran for thirty one days instead of eleven, and delivered forty four leads at a stable seven dollars each. Thirty nine of them left a phone number. Twenty one said they want to start inside three months.

Three of those forty four ever opened the training video. No lead status was set, no note was written and no call was logged all month. There were no sales. The traffic is not the problem any more.

Click-through rate went from 7.66% to 11.77%. The creative is working considerably harder than it was. People who see these ads click them.
But reach halved, 5,506 to 2,815, and the thousand-impression price nearly doubled, $36.31 to $67.19. We paid more for a smaller audience.
Those two cancelled out. A better advert on more expensive inventory lands within sixty cents of last month's cost per lead. That is the whole paid story.
Weekly cost per lead stayed inside a narrow band: $6.15, $9.46, $5.97, $7.22. There is no decline here, just normal week to week noise.

This is not a tracking fault. We checked. The SKLES pixel is installed on the blueprint page, it fires a page view, and Meta recorded it firing as recently as 31 October. The number is real.
The form's thank-you screen is the leak. Someone finishes the form, sees a panel saying "Watch the training", and closes it. The video they came for sits one unnecessary click away.
248 people did watch a full advert to the end inside Facebook. The appetite for the content is there. It just never survives the handover to our own page.
Everything we built downstream is waiting on this. The video, the landing page, the $8.99 offer, the email sequence. None of it gets a fair test while three people a month arrive.
What this costs us. At $7.02 a lead we are paying roughly $103 for each person who actually reaches the training video. Fixing the handover does not need more budget, a new advert or a new video. It needs the thank-you panel to stop being a door.

October · Email

The sequence is being opened. It is not being clicked.

The Fast Track sequence

  • 108 emails sent to the 38 leads who reached GetResponse. 28 finished the sequence, 10 are still inside it.
  • 19 opens, by 10 different people. Roughly one lead in four opens something. That is a workable open rate for a cold list.
  • One click. All month. From 108 sends. This is the number that matters and it is the same story as the landing page.
  • The September worry is settled. We reported that only two leads had ever opened a message. With a full month of data that reads as ten of thirty eight, and both sending domains are properly authenticated.

The broadcasts

  • 12 broadcasts went out, 91 emails in total, to a median of seven people per send. September sent 502.
  • Every single one promoted a live session. Debt payoff on four dates, contractor questions on three.
  • No webinar ran on the Zoom account in October. The last one was 29 September. If these sessions happened somewhere else, we need to know where, because we cannot see attendance.
  • 54 opens across the 12 sends. The rates look strong, but they are the arithmetic of a list of seven.

November · The plan

Five things, in order of what they are worth.

  • Send the form straight into the video. Three of forty four opened the training. Remove the thank-you panel as a stop: either auto-redirect on submit, or put the video on the panel itself. This is a day of work and it is worth more than every other item on this page combined.
  • Call the twenty one. They have phone numbers, they said three months, and the queue is already built and already sorted. Rodney needs to open it and work down the list. A single conversation will teach us more than another month of adverts.
  • Put the budget behind "The objection". It turns more than a third of its clicks into leads, at $6.14, against 16.7% and $7.63 for the migraine advert. Keep the migraine creative for reach and let the objection creative do the converting.
  • Stop emailing about live sessions. Twelve of twelve broadcasts promoted a session that does not appear on the Zoom account. Point those sends at the training video instead, so the one email a week this list gets has somewhere real to go.
  • Then raise the spend. At a cost per lead that has not moved in two months, more money buys more leads. We just should not buy more of them until the forty we already have are phoned.
